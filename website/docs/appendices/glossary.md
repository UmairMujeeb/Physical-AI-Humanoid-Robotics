# Glossary of Technical Terms

This glossary provides definitions for technical terms used throughout the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics book. Understanding these terms is essential for following the concepts and implementations discussed in the modules.

## A

**Articulation**: A joint or connection between rigid bodies in a robotic system that allows relative motion between them.

**Autonomous System**: A system that can operate independently without human intervention, making decisions based on its programming and sensor inputs.

## B

**Behavior Tree**: A hierarchical structure used in robotics and AI to organize and execute complex behaviors in a modular way.

**Bounding Box**: A rectangular box that encloses an object in 2D or 3D space, used for object detection and collision detection.

## C

**Computer Vision**: A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world through digital images and videos.

**Control Loop**: A feedback mechanism that continuously adjusts system behavior based on sensor input to achieve desired outputs.

**Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)**: A class of deep neural networks commonly used for analyzing visual imagery.

**Coordinate Frame**: A system of axes used to define positions and orientations in space, typically using X, Y, and Z axes.

## D

**Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning based on artificial neural networks with representation learning.

**Depth Image**: An image where each pixel value represents the distance from the camera to the object at that pixel location.

**Dexterity**: The capability of a robotic hand or manipulator to perform fine manipulation tasks.

**DH Parameters**: Denavit-Hartenberg parameters used to define the kinematic structure of robotic manipulators.

**Domain Randomization**: A technique in simulation where random variations are added to the environment to improve the transfer of learning from simulation to reality.

## E

**End Effector**: The tool or device at the end of a robotic arm that interacts with the environment, such as a gripper or tool.

**Euclidean Distance**: The straight-line distance between two points in Euclidean space.

**Ethical AI**: The development and deployment of artificial intelligence systems that are fair, transparent, and accountable.

## F

**Forward Kinematics**: The process of determining the position and orientation of the end effector given the joint angles of a robotic manipulator.

**Force Control**: A control strategy that regulates the forces applied by a robot during interaction with objects or environments.

**Fused Perception**: The integration of multiple sensory inputs to create a more robust understanding of the environment.

## G

**Gazebo**: A robotics simulator that provides realistic physics simulation and rendering for testing robotic algorithms.

**Gripper**: A device at the end of a robotic arm used to grasp and manipulate objects.

**Grasp Planning**: The process of determining how to grasp an object based on its shape, size, and other properties.

## H

**Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL)**: A testing methodology that involves connecting actual hardware components to a simulation environment.

**Humanoid Robot**: A robot with a human-like body structure, typically featuring a head, torso, two arms, and two legs.

## I

**Impedance Control**: A control method that regulates the dynamic relationship between force and motion to achieve desired interaction behavior.

**Inverse Kinematics**: The process of determining the joint angles required to achieve a desired position and orientation of the end effector.

**Isaac Sim**: NVIDIA's robotics simulation environment built on the Omniverse platform.

**Isaac SDK**: NVIDIA's robotics software development kit for building AI-powered robots.

## J

**Joint Space**: The space defined by the joint angles of a robotic manipulator.

**Jacobian Matrix**: A matrix that describes the relationship between joint velocities and end-effector velocities in robotics.

## K

**Kinematics**: The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it, particularly the motion of robotic mechanisms.

## L

**LiDAR**: Light Detection and Ranging, a remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

**Lidar Sensor**: A sensor that uses laser light to measure distances and create 3D maps of the environment.

**Machine Learning**: A method of training algorithms to learn patterns from data and make predictions or decisions.

**Manipulation**: The ability of a robot to physically interact with objects in its environment.

**Marker Detection**: The process of identifying and tracking visual markers in images or video streams.

**Motion Planning**: The process of determining a sequence of movements to achieve a goal while avoiding obstacles.

## N

**Navigation**: The ability of a robot to move from one location to another in its environment.

**Neural Network**: A computing system inspired by the human brain, consisting of interconnected nodes that process information.

## O

**Omniverse**: NVIDIA's simulation and collaboration platform for 3D design workflows.

**OpenCV**: An open-source computer vision and machine learning software library.

**Operational Space**: The space in which the end-effector of a robot operates, typically Cartesian space.

**Overfitting**: A machine learning problem where a model learns the training data too well, including noise and outliers.

## P

**Path Planning**: The process of finding a sequence of positions that a robot should follow to reach a goal.

**Perception**: The ability of a robot to interpret sensory data to understand its environment.

**PID Controller**: A control loop mechanism employing proportional, integral, and derivative terms to minimize error.

**Point Cloud**: A set of data points in space, typically representing the external surface of an object.

**Pose Estimation**: The process of determining the position and orientation of an object or robot in space.

**Prismatic Joint**: A joint that allows linear motion along a single axis.

**Probabilistic Robotics**: An approach to robotics that explicitly accounts for uncertainty in sensing and actuation.

## Q

**Quaternion**: A mathematical representation of rotation in 3D space, avoiding issues like gimbal lock.

## R

**Reinforcement Learning**: A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by receiving rewards or penalties.

**Revolute Joint**: A joint that allows rotational motion around a single axis.

**Robot Operating System (ROS)**: Flexible framework for writing robot software that provides services for hardware abstraction and device drivers.

**ROS 2**: The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved security and real-time capabilities.

**RGB-D Camera**: A camera that captures both color (RGB) and depth (D) information.

**Rigid Body**: An idealization of a solid body in which deformation is neglected, used in physics simulation.

**Rolling Shutter**: A method of image capture where the image is captured line by line across the sensor rather than all at once.

## S

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining data from multiple sensors to improve the accuracy and reliability of information.

**Simulation-to-Reality Transfer**: The process of transferring skills or behaviors learned in simulation to real-world robots.

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location within it.

**Stereovision**: The process of extracting 3D information from 2D images captured from two or more viewpoints.

**Surgical Robot**: A robot designed to assist in surgical procedures, typically featuring high precision and dexterity.

## T

**Task Planning**: The process of creating a sequence of actions to achieve a high-level goal.

**Teaching by Demonstration**: A method where a robot learns tasks by observing and mimicking human demonstrations.

**Trajectory**: A path that an object follows through space as a function of time.

**Transform**: A mathematical operation that converts coordinates from one coordinate frame to another.

**Trustworthy AI**: AI systems that are robust, reliable, fair, and transparent in their operations.

## V

**VLA (Vision Language Action)**: Models that combine visual perception, language understanding, and action generation in robotics.

**Velocity Control**: A control method that regulates the speed of robotic joints or end-effectors.

**Virtual Reality (VR)**: A computer-generated simulation of a three-dimensional environment that can be interacted with in a seemingly real way.

**Vision System**: A system that processes visual information to enable robots to perceive and interpret their environment.

## W

**Workspace**: The volume of space that a robot's end effector can reach.

**World Coordinate System**: A fixed reference frame used to define positions and orientations in the environment.

## Y

**Yaw**: The rotation of an object around its vertical axis.

## Z

**Zero Moment Point (ZMP)**: A concept used in robotics to assess the stability of legged robots during walking or standing.

**Z-Buffer**: A component of 3D rendering that handles the depth of objects to determine visibility.
