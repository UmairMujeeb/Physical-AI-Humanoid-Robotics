# Curriculum Mapping for Different Educational Contexts

This appendix provides detailed curriculum mappings that align the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics content with various educational frameworks, standards, and institutional requirements. The mappings help educators understand how the content fits into broader educational contexts and can be adapted for different settings.

## Overview

Curriculum mapping is essential for ensuring that the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics content aligns with institutional goals, accreditation requirements, and educational standards. This document provides mappings for different educational contexts including K-12, undergraduate, graduate, and professional development settings.

## Mapping Framework

### Educational Standards Alignment

#### Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) - K-12
The content aligns with several NGSS dimensions:

**Science and Engineering Practices**:
- Asking questions and defining problems
- Developing and using models
- Planning and carrying out investigations
- Analyzing and interpreting data
- Using mathematics and computational thinking
- Constructing explanations and designing solutions
- Engaging in argument from evidence
- Obtaining, evaluating, and communicating information

**Crosscutting Concepts**:
- Patterns
- Cause and effect
- Scale, proportion, and quantity
- Systems and system models
- Energy and matter
- Structure and function
- Stability and change

**Disciplinary Core Ideas**:
- ETS1: Engineering Design
- PS2: Motion and Stability: Forces and Interactions
- PS3: Energy
- LS1: From Molecules to Organisms: Structures and Processes

#### ABET Engineering Criteria (Undergraduate)

- (a) Apply engineering knowledge

## K-12 Education Mapping

### Middle School (Grades 6-8)

#### Learning Objectives
- Understand basic robotics concepts and applications
- Develop computational thinking skills
- Explore engineering design processes
- Connect robotics to real-world applications

#### Module Adaptation
- **Introduction Module**: Focus on basic concepts and applications
- **ROS 2 Basics**: Simplified to basic programming concepts
- **Gazebo Introduction**: Emphasis on visual programming and simulation
- **NVIDIA Isaac**: Introduction to AI concepts through visual examples

#### Time Allocation (Semester Course)
- **Weeks 1-3**: Introduction to Robotics and Basic Concepts
- **Weeks 4-7**: Simple Programming and Simulation
- **Weeks 8-11**: Basic Robot Control and Navigation
- **Weeks 12-15**: Simple AI and Perception Concepts
- **Weeks 16-18**: Final Project and Presentations

#### Assessment Strategies
- **Project-Based**: Simple robot programming projects
- **Portfolio**: Collection of programming exercises
- **Peer Presentations**: Students demonstrate their robots
- **Reflection Journals**: Document learning and challenges

### High School (Grades 9-12)

#### Learning Objectives
- Develop programming and computational skills
- Understand engineering design processes
- Apply mathematics and science to robotics
- Prepare for STEM careers and college

#### Module Adaptation
- **Introduction Module**: Comprehensive overview with career connections
- **ROS 2 Basics**: Full implementation with advanced programming
- **Gazebo Introduction**: Complex simulation scenarios
- **NVIDIA Isaac**: Advanced AI and machine learning concepts

#### Time Allocation (Full Year Course)
- **Semester 1**:
  - Weeks 1-4: Introduction and ROS 2 fundamentals
  - Weeks 5-9: Basic robot programming and control
  - Weeks 10-14: Navigation and path planning
  - Weeks 15-18: Midterm project and presentations

- **Semester 2**:
  - Weeks 1-5: Perception and computer vision basics
  - Weeks 6-10: Manipulation and control systems
  - Weeks 11-15: Advanced AI concepts
  - Weeks 16-18: Final project and presentations

#### Assessment Strategies
- **Technical Projects**: Programming and simulation challenges
- **Written Exams**: Theoretical concepts and problem solving
- **Laboratory Reports**: Documentation of experiments
- **Capstone Project**: Comprehensive robotics challenge

## International Education Mapping

### Different Educational Systems

#### European Credit Transfer System (ECTS)
- **Bachelor's Level**: 5-6 ECTS per module
- **Master's Level**: 6-8 ECTS per module
- **Assessment**: Continuous assessment with final examination

#### International Baccalaureate (IB)
- **Higher Level**: 240 hours of instruction
- **Standard Level**: 150 hours of instruction
- **Internal Assessment**: 20% of final grade
- **External Assessment**: 80% of final grade

#### A-Level Curriculum (UK)
- **AS Level**: Foundational robotics concepts
- **A2 Level**: Advanced implementation and theory
- **Coursework**: 20-30% of final grade
- **Examinations**: 70-80% of final grade

## Learning Outcome Mapping

### Cognitive Domain (Bloom's Taxonomy)

#### Remember
- Define key robotics terms and concepts
- Recall basic ROS 2 commands and structures
- Identify different types of sensors and actuators

#### Understand
- Explain how robot systems integrate perception and action
- Describe the principles of robot navigation
- Summarize the role of AI in robotics

#### Apply
- Implement basic ROS 2 nodes and communication
- Program robot navigation in simulation
- Apply control algorithms to robot systems

#### Analyze
- Compare different robot architectures and approaches
- Evaluate the performance of robot systems
- Diagnose problems in robot implementations

#### Evaluate
- Assess the effectiveness of different algorithms
- Critique robot system designs
- Justify design choices in robot implementations

#### Create
- Design new robot applications and systems
- Develop novel approaches to robotics problems
- Integrate multiple robotics concepts into solutions

### Affective Domain
- **Receiving**: Showing willingness to learn robotics concepts
- **Responding**: Participating actively in robotics activities
- **Valuing**: Appreciating the importance of robotics
- **Organization**: Integrating robotics values with other values
- **Characterization**: Becoming a robotics professional

### Psychomotor Domain
- **Perception**: Using sensory cues in robotics tasks
- **Set**: Readiness to act in robotics situations
- **Guided Response**: Imitating robotics procedures
- **Mechanism**: Performing robotics tasks with confidence
- **Complex Overt Response**: Complex robotics implementations
- **Adaptation**: Adapting to new robotics challenges
- **Origination**: Creating new robotics solutions
